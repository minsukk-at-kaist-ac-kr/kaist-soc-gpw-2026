2026 Program Results

A Week of Research, Community, and Exploration

From September 7–11, 31 visiting students took part in a program built around direct exposure to KAIST School of Computing: faculty research, laboratories, graduate students, admissions, campus life, and the broader experience of studying in Korea. Rather than a lecture-only preview, GPW emphasized small-group conversations and first-hand research experiences.

Monday · Welcome to KAIST

Starting with people, place, and research

The week opened with a welcome session and an introduction to KAIST SoC, followed by participant introductions and a welcome lunch with faculty. Social activities and campus exploration helped the group get comfortable quickly, while the first faculty talks introduced the research culture that would continue throughout the week.

The opening day was designed to make faculty interaction feel accessible from the start and to give participants an immediate sense of both the academic and social environment.

Tuesday & Wednesday · Research Immersion

From faculty talks to small-group lab visits

Morning research talks exposed participants to a broad range of computing research, from cybersecurity, generative AI, robotics, and theoretical computer science to software security and embodied intelligence. Afternoons shifted to lab visits, where smaller groups could see research spaces and speak directly with faculty members and graduate researchers.

Lunch with KAIST graduate students and informal activities created another channel for candid conversations about research life, graduate study, and living at KAIST.

Thursday · Research & Closing

Admissions, Security@KAIST, and final research connections

Thursday brought together a discussion of KAIST SoC graduate study and admissions, the Security@KAIST poster session, another faculty research talk, and final lab visits. Participants could move from broad program information to direct conversations with student researchers and research groups before the closing session.

Friday · Seoul Experience

Ending the week with a shared cultural experience

After four research-focused days in Daejeon, the group spent Friday in Seoul. The cultural program gave participants time to experience Korea together, strengthen friendships formed during the week, and close GPW on a shared experience beyond campus.

Graduate-school pathway

75% selected a current path that explicitly included KAIST graduate study: 15% chose KAIST graduate school and 60% were considering both KAIST and other graduate schools.

Another 15% were not yet sure, while 5% selected graduate school elsewhere and 5% employment/industry.

What we learned for future programs

Participants asked for even more interaction

The most common suggestions were more social/cultural activities (75%), more faculty interaction (50%), more graduate-student interaction (45%), and more lab visits (40%). These responses reinforce the value of the program's small-group and relationship-oriented components.

Key concerns about future study

The most frequently selected concerns were the language barrier (55%), career opportunities after graduation (45%), and cultural adjustment (40%). Future recruiting and follow-up programs can address these questions more directly.

About Global Preview Week

KAIST SoC Global Preview Week is an invitation-based program designed to introduce selected students to the research environment, graduate programs, faculty, laboratories, graduate students, and campus life of KAIST School of Computing.

The main program runs at KAIST in Daejeon from Monday through Thursday, followed by a one-day Seoul experience on Friday.

2026 Cohort

The inaugural 2026 program brought 31 selected students from partner universities in Taiwan, Vietnam, Thailand, and Malaysia to KAIST for an intensive week of research and community engagement.

Main program: September 7–11, 2026 · Daejeon and Seoul
